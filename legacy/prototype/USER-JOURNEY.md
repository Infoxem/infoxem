# Infoxem: the user's journey

This guide explains what users do, what they see, and what happens next. Infoxem currently uses fictional information for demonstration; searching and requesting data do not contact live services.

## 1. Home: choose where to begin

The exporter opens Infoxem and chooses **Companies** to look for business partners or **People** to find relevant contacts. They can also begin with a company list they already have through **Data Enrichment**.

## 2. Companies: search for potential business partners

The Companies screen initially shows no company results. The exporter enters a medicine, product category, country, or combined search such as **Paracetamol in Kenya**, then selects **Search** or presses Enter.

Infoxem displays matching companies. The exporter can then narrow the list by company name, country, county or city, company size, industry, and business keywords. A Kenya search enables county selection. They select **Apply filters** to update the results.

Changing a search or filter does not immediately replace the list. **Unapplied changes** means the displayed results still belong to the previous search. The applied criteria above the list show what the current results represent.

The list contains five useful columns:

- **Company:** name, website, size range, and a save bookmark.
- **Business type:** distributor, wholesaler, or retailer, plus known contacts.
- **Location:** city, county when available, and country.
- **Industry and products:** the company's industry and medicine categories.
- **Company contact:** email, phone, and a missing-data request option when needed.

The exporter can sort results, move between pages, save a company, or click its name to explore further. If nothing matches, they can broaden the search or clear the filters and try again.

## 3. Company details: decide whether the company is relevant

Clicking a company name opens its profile. The exporter first sees **Overview**, including the business type, location, size, industry, description, product categories, and available company contact details.

The **Employees** tab lists known employees and their job titles, locations, email addresses, phone numbers, and LinkedIn profile availability. Known employee contacts do not represent the company's entire workforce.

The exporter can save the company, save a particular employee, or request missing information. **Back** returns them to the previous results and the same Discover or Saved tab.

## 4. People: find the person to speak to

The People screen also starts without results. The exporter searches by medicine/product category or country, then refines by company name, job title, location, industry, and professional keywords.

For example, they search **antimalarial drugs in Kenya**, enter **Procurement Manager**, and select **Apply filters**. Medicine searches identify people through the products associated with their employers; they do not imply that a person has a particular medical qualification or expertise.

The People list shows the person's name and role, employer, location, available work contacts, and LinkedIn profile availability. The exporter can save a person or click their name to inspect the full professional profile.

## 5. Person details: review professional contact information

The exporter opens a person's profile to review their role, employer, location, industry, professional keywords, available email, phone, and LinkedIn information. They can save this person or request missing contact details.

In this mockup, LinkedIn buttons open an illustrative profile explanation. They do not open a verified real LinkedIn account.

## 6. Discover and Saved: build a shortlist

Each People and Companies screen contains two tabs: **Discover** and **Saved**. There is no separate Saved screen in the sidebar.

The exporter bookmarks a result to keep it. Saved companies appear only in the Companies screen's Saved tab. Saved people appear only in the People screen's Saved tab. Saving a company does not automatically save its employees, and saving a person does not automatically save their employer.

The exporter can search and refine their Saved tab without searching all discovery records again. Removing a bookmark removes that record from Saved; it does not delete it from search results.

In the demo, bookmarks remain available when the same browser is reopened or refreshed, provided browser storage is available. If storage is unavailable, the platform reports that the save lasts only for the current session.

## 7. Request Data: ask for information that is missing

When contact or company information is unavailable, the exporter selects **Request Data**. They see the missing fields for that company or person, choose what they need, and can add a note explaining the request.

After submitting, they see a request reference and **Pending** status. Previously requested fields are marked pending so they do not request the same field twice. They can still request another missing field later.

This is the end of the demonstrated request journey. Requests are recorded locally; the mockup does not perform research, deliver new information, or promise a completion time.

## 8. Data Enrichment: start with a list the exporter already has

The exporter chooses **Data Enrichment**, names their list, and selects a company CSV file or the provided sample. Infoxem shows a small preview so they can check that they have chosen the right list.

They select the information they want: contact details, email addresses, company addresses, LinkedIn profiles, or another company detail. Infoxem asks follow-up questions relevant to those choices, such as target roles, contact count, geography, email type, and address detail.

The exporter reviews the complete request, makes any corrections, and selects **Start enrichment**.

They see progress through company matching, finding information, checking quality, saving results, and preparing the workspace. In this mockup, these stages are simulated.

When the process finishes, **View enriched companies** opens the completed list. Missing information remains clearly marked; unavailable or unverified information is not presented as confirmed.

## 9. Enriched Companies: review an enriched list

The exporter sees their completed list with coverage and completeness indicators. They can search, filter by country or completion status, sort the list, and select a company to inspect its available information.

They can revisit their requirements or begin another enrichment. This workspace contains the list they imported and enriched; it is separate from searching for new companies under Find Leads.

## Example journey for the Kenya enquiry

1. Open **Companies** and search **Paracetamol in Kenya**.
2. Narrow the results to the relevant county, industry, or company-size range.
3. Open a company and review its business type and product categories.
4. Inspect **Employees** or open **People** to find a procurement contact.
5. Save suitable companies and people to their respective **Saved** tabs.
6. Use **Request Data** for missing company or employee contact information.

All companies, contacts, product associations, company sizes, and contact values currently shown are fictional examples. This journey demonstrates the intended experience, rather than a verified supplier directory or a live research service.
